Setting up your Amazon Associates account to become an Amazon affiliate is quite straightforward and easy — once you know exactly what to expect. You might be scratching your head right now, asking yourself: *"Why would you make me build my website first if this whole process is supposed to be easy?"* Well, that's because Amazon requires you to have a live website (or app) **before** you can be approved as an associate. Without an active platform to review, your application simply won't go through.

In this guide, we'll walk through every step of the sign up process, explain what Amazon is actually looking for at each stage, and share a few practical tips to help you get approved faster — and stay approved.

## Why Amazon Requires a Website First

Amazon's affiliate program isn't just a formality — it's a real partnership. Amazon wants to know that the traffic sending visitors to their marketplace is legitimate, relevant, and not going to violate their terms of service. That's why the application asks so many detailed questions about your site's content, traffic sources, and monetization strategy.

If you're building a blog like TechWeMate or any other content site, this means you should have at least a handful of published articles and a clear niche focus before applying. A bare, empty WordPress or Astro installation with no content is likely to get rejected.

## Getting Started: What You Need Before You Apply

Before starting the sign up process, make sure you have the following ready:

- **Your website data.** This includes your domain name, a clear description of your site's purpose, and information about any other affiliate programs already featured on your site (if applicable).
- **An Amazon.com account.** If you want to keep your affiliate activities separate from your personal shopping account, it's a good idea to create a brand new Amazon account specifically for this purpose. This keeps your business finances and personal purchases cleanly separated — something that will save you headaches later when it's time to track commissions or file taxes.

It's also worth having a rough idea of your monthly traffic numbers and how you plan to drive visitors to your site (SEO, social media, YouTube, email marketing, etc.), since you'll be asked about this during sign up.

## Sign Up and Set Up: The Full Walkthrough

### Step 1: Start the Application

Go to the Amazon homepage and scroll all the way down to reach the **"Make Money with Us"** section. Click on **"Become an Affiliate."** On the following page, click **"Join for Free."**

For the purposes of this walkthrough, we'll assume you already have an existing Amazon.com account, so we'll sign up using that account rather than creating a brand new one from scratch.

### Step 2: Enter Your Account Information

Sign up using your email address, then click **"Sign up using our secure server."** The first setup step asks you to enter your **account information** — specifically, your payee details. This is the personal information of whoever will be receiving payments for the commissions earned through your affiliate website.

By default, Amazon will pull in your existing personal information — full name, address, and phone number — but you can change any of these by clicking **"Select different address"** if your payment details differ from your account profile.

### Step 3: Confirm the Main Contact

In the **"Who is the main contact for this account?"** section, you'll decide whether the payee should also be the main point of contact, or whether you want to appoint someone else using the **"Someone Else"** option. Once this is set, click **"Next."**

### Step 4: Add Your Websites and Apps

You're now in the **"Your Websites and Mobile Apps"** section. Here, you need to enter at least one website or mobile app where you'll be displaying affiliate links — and you can add up to 50 in total, which is handy if you're running multiple niche sites.

Simply type your domain name into the **"Enter your website"** field and click **"Add."** Once you're done adding your site(s), click **"Next."**

Before continuing, Amazon will ask you to confirm whether any of your websites or apps are primarily directed at children under the age of 13. This matters because such sites are **not eligible** to display Amazon Associates links, banners, or ads under COPPA regulations. Check **"No"** (assuming this applies to you) and click **"Confirm"** to continue.

### Step 5: Build Your Affiliate Profile

Next, you'll land on the **"Profile"** section, where Amazon asks you to build out a profile for your affiliate website.

- **Associates Store ID:** Choose something simple and memorable — ideally related to your brand name (for example, "techwemate-20").
- **Website description:** Write a brief, honest description of what your site or app is about in the **"What are your websites or mobile apps about?"** field.

### Step 6: Choose Your Topics and Product Categories

In the **"Which of the following topics best describes your websites or mobile apps?"** section, select a primary and secondary category that best fits the content you publish. Choose categories that genuinely reflect your niche — Amazon uses this to match your account with the type of products you're likely to promote.

Next, in **"What type of Amazon items do you intend to list on your websites or mobile apps?,"** check every product category you realistically expect to feature in your content, whether that's electronics, books, home goods, or software tools.

### Step 7: Describe Your Website or App Type

In the **"What type are your websites or mobile apps?"** section, again select a primary and secondary category — this time describing the *format* of your platform (blog, review site, comparison site, YouTube channel, mobile app, etc.).

### Step 8: Traffic and Monetization Details

This is where Amazon digs a little deeper into how your business actually works.

In **"How do you drive traffic to your websites?,"** check every option that applies to your actual traffic strategy — SEO, paid ads, social media, email marketing, YouTube, and so on. Be honest here; Amazon cross-checks this information against your actual site performance over time.

In **"How do you utilize your websites and apps to generate income?,"** select every other monetization method you use alongside Amazon Associates — display ads (like AdSense), other affiliate programs, digital product sales, sponsorships, etc. If Amazon Associates will be your only monetization method for now, simply select **"Amazon Associates is the only way I monetize my site."**

### Step 9: Final Details

Fill in the remaining fields according to your own situation:

- **How do you usually build links?**
- **How many total unique visitors do your websites and apps get per month?**
- **What is your primary reason for joining the Amazon Associates Program?**
- **How did you hear about us?**

Answer these as accurately as possible — inflating your traffic numbers won't help you and could actually raise red flags during Amazon's review process.

### Step 10: Verification and Confirmation

Finally, enter the security code provided and click **"Next"** to move to the last section. You'll receive a confirmation that your identity verification has been completed. Check the box under **"Contract Terms"** to accept the Operating Agreement, then click **"Finish"** to complete your application.

## What Happens After You Apply

Once your application is submitted, you're officially enrolled — but there's an important catch: Amazon gives you **180 days** to generate your first qualifying sale. If you don't make at least one sale within that window, your account may be closed, and you'll need to reapply.

This is exactly why it's so important to have your website already publishing content and attracting at least a small amount of organic traffic before you apply. The clock starts ticking the moment you're approved, not when you're ready.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Applying too early.** A site with one or two thin articles rarely gets approved, and even if it does, generating a sale within 180 days will be nearly impossible without existing traffic.
- **Ignoring the disclosure requirements.** Once approved, you're required to clearly disclose your affiliate relationship with Amazon on every page containing affiliate links (a simple line like *"As an Amazon Associate, I earn from qualifying purchases"* is standard).
- **Inconsistent link placement.** Amazon tracks performance closely — random, unrelated links dropped into content that don't match the surrounding topic tend to underperform and can look spammy to both readers and Amazon's review team.
- **Forgetting to link your tracking ID correctly.** Every link needs to include your unique Associates ID so commissions are properly attributed to your account.

## Final Thoughts

Setting up your Amazon Associates account is a fairly mechanical process once you understand what each section is asking for — but the real work happens *before* you even start the application. Having a focused website with real content, a clear niche, and at least some traffic in place will make the difference between getting approved and getting rejected, and more importantly, between earning your first commission within 180 days or losing your account before you even get started.

Take the time to build your foundation first. The sign up form itself will feel like the easy part.
